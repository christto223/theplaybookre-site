Cold calling is the most direct path to a real estate pipeline that exists. It is also the activity that most new agents abandon before it has a chance to work.

The reason isn't that cold calling doesn't work. It's that most agents approach it without a system — they dial for a few weeks, get rejected, don't see results, and conclude that cold calling must not be for them.

What they're actually missing is the compounding effect. A cold calling pipeline doesn't produce results linearly. It produces them exponentially, after a lag period that most agents don't survive.

This playbook is about surviving that lag period and building the habit that makes it work.

## What You Actually Need to Start

The barrier to starting a cold calling practice is lower than most new agents think. You need:

- A phone
- A contact list (we'll build this)
- A script (we'll cover this)
- A tracking system (we'll set this up)
- 90 minutes a day

That's it. You don't need an expensive dialer on day one. You don't need a CRM with automated sequences. You don't need a professional recording setup or a dedicated office. You need a phone, a list, and the discipline to show up.

:::callout
**The 90-Day Rule:** Cold calling produces almost no visible results in the first 30 days. It produces some results in days 31–60. It starts to compound in days 61–90. Most agents quit somewhere in days 15–45, which is right before the system starts to work. Commit to 90 days before you evaluate.
:::

## Building Your First Contact List

Your first list is not a purchased lead list. Purchased lead lists are useful eventually — they're not where you start.

Your first list is every person within a 5-mile radius of your office who owns their home. This is public record information. Here's how to get it:

1. Go to your county assessor's website
2. Search by zip code or neighborhood
3. Download or export the owner list (most counties allow this)
4. Import into a spreadsheet with: name, address, phone (if available)

If phone numbers aren't available from the assessor, you can use a skip tracing service to append them. Services like BatchDialer or PropStream offer skip tracing at low per-record costs.

Your first list should be 500–1,000 contacts. That sounds like a lot. At 50 dials per day, that's 10–20 days of calling before you need to refill it. You'll add to it constantly.

## The Opening Script That Actually Works

Most cold calling scripts fail for the same reason: they sound like cold calling scripts. The person picks up, hears the cadence, and hangs up before you finish the first sentence.

The script that works sounds like a neighbor, not a salesperson.

**Opening:**
> "Hi, is this [Name]? ... Hey, this is [Your Name] — I'm a real estate agent in [neighborhood/area]. I'm not calling to list your home or anything like that. I just noticed you've been in the neighborhood for a while and wanted to introduce myself. Do you have 30 seconds?"

Most people say yes to 30 seconds. The key elements here:
- You led with your name and what you are (transparency)
- You preemptively addressed the obvious concern (I'm not calling to pitch you)
- You asked for a specific, small commitment (30 seconds, not "a few minutes")

**The 30-Second Pitch:**
> "I'm working this neighborhood pretty heavily right now. A couple of houses have sold recently and I've been having conversations with homeowners just to stay connected to the market. No agenda — I just like to know who's out there. Have you given any thought to your home in the last year or so?"

This is a conversation-opener, not a close. You're gathering information and building a relationship. 

## Tracking Your Numbers

You cannot improve what you don't measure. Every session, you need to track:

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|--------|------------------|
| Dials | Raw activity — are you doing the work? |
| Contacts (live answers) | Contact rate — is your list good? |
| Conversations (2+ min) | Engagement rate — is your script working? |
| Follow-up appointments set | Conversion rate — are you asking correctly? |

Target benchmarks for a new agent in the first 90 days:
- Contact rate: 10–15% of dials (1 live answer per 7–10 dials)
- Conversation rate: 50% of contacts (half of people who answer will talk)
- Appointment rate: 5–10% of conversations (1 appointment per 10–20 conversations)

At 50 dials per day, 5 days per week: you should be having 3–5 real conversations per day and setting roughly 1–2 listing appointments per week by month two.

## The Follow-Up System That Creates Pipeline

Cold calling is not a one-touch strategy. The pipeline is built in the follow-up.

:::phase
### Phase 1: Initial Contact (Day 1)
Complete the call. If positive, note the conversation details immediately. If no answer, add to callback list.

### Phase 2: First Follow-Up (Day 7–10)
Reference the previous conversation specifically. "We spoke about a week ago — you mentioned you'd been thinking about upsizing." Specificity = trust.

### Phase 3: Ongoing Nurture (Every 30–60 Days)
Market update calls. "I'm calling because a home similar to yours just sold at [price]. Wanted to make sure you had that information." Value-first, low pressure.

### Phase 4: Appointment Conversion (When Ready)
By this point, you have a relationship. The appointment conversation is a natural next step, not a cold close.
:::

## FAQ

**How many dials should I do per day?**
Start with 30–50 dials per day. That's achievable in 90 minutes. Once you've built the habit, scale to 75–100 dials if your schedule allows. Quality of list and script matter more than raw volume.

**What's the best time of day to cold call?**
Tuesday through Thursday, 9–11am and 4–6pm, produce the highest contact rates consistently. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons. Avoid dinner hours (6–7pm) entirely.

**Should I use a dialer or call from my cell?**
Start with your cell phone. A dialer adds complexity before you've built the fundamental habit. Once you're consistently hitting 50+ dials per day for 30+ days, evaluate a power dialer.

**How do I handle "I'm not interested"?**
"Totally fair — I appreciate you telling me directly. Would it be okay if I followed up in six months or so, just to stay in touch?" Most people say yes to six months. You've just kept the relationship open.

**What about the Do Not Call registry?**
Check every number against the National DNC Registry before you dial. Violations carry significant fines. Most dialers and skip-tracing services offer DNC scrubbing. Do not skip this step.
