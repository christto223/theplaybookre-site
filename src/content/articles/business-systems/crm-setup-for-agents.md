The average real estate agent has hundreds of contacts in their phone, a half-built CRM they haven't logged into in three months, and a folder of sticky notes with follow-up reminders they'll get to eventually.

The pipeline leaks constantly. Good contacts go cold. Deals that should have happened don't. Referrals go to someone else because you forgot to stay in touch.

A properly configured CRM stops the leak. This playbook is about setting yours up in a way that actually gets used.

## The Problem With Most CRM Setups

Most agents approach their CRM like a database — a place to store contact information. That's half of what a CRM is supposed to do. The other half is a task management system that tells you who to call, when, and why.

When agents set up a CRM as a database only, they end up with a very expensive address book. The follow-up still lives in their head, which means it doesn't happen consistently.

The setup that works treats every contact as having a status, a next action, and a date for that next action. The CRM tells you what to do today. You don't have to remember.

## The Three Contact Categories

Before you configure anything, understand that your contacts fall into three categories that require completely different follow-up approaches:

:::callout
**Active Pipeline** — People who are actively buying or selling in the next 90 days. These contacts need attention multiple times per week. Your CRM should flag these people at the top of your daily workflow.

**Warm Nurture** — People who are thinking about buying or selling but not yet ready to act. 30–90 day follow-up cadence. The goal is to stay top of mind and build trust until they're ready.

**Long-Term Database** — Everyone else. Sphere of influence, past clients, cold prospects. 60–90 day touch minimum. The goal is to not become a stranger.
:::

## The Contact Record Setup

Every contact record needs five things to be useful:

| Field | What Goes Here | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Full name + phone + email | Complete contact info | Obvious, but do it right |
| Source | How you met them | Tells you what messaging resonates |
| Status | Active / Warm / Database | Drives your follow-up frequency |
| Last contact date | When you last spoke | Prevents contacts going cold |
| Next action + date | What to do and when | This is what makes the CRM work |

The "Next Action + Date" field is the most important field in your CRM and the one most agents leave blank.

## The Follow-Up Sequences

Set up these three automated or semi-automated sequences:

**New Contact Sequence (Days 1–14)**
- Day 1: Introductory call or connect
- Day 3: Text or email with something relevant (market data, article, etc.)
- Day 7: Follow-up call
- Day 14: Check-in call — "I've been thinking about what you mentioned..."

**Active Pipeline Sequence**
- Every 2–3 days: some form of contact (call, text, email)
- Weekly: substantive conversation about timeline and needs
- As needed: property updates, showing feedback, offer updates

**Long-Term Nurture Sequence**
- Every 60 days: phone call
- Monthly: market update email (automated from your email platform)
- Annually: "thinking of you" call around their home anniversary

## The Daily CRM Workflow

The CRM only works if you open it every day. Build this 15-minute habit at the start of your business day:

1. Review today's follow-up tasks (these should be pre-populated by your sequences)
2. Complete any calls or texts from yesterday you didn't finish
3. Update any contact records from yesterday's conversations
4. Set next actions for any contacts you spoke with

Fifteen minutes. Every business day. The pipeline stops leaking.

## FAQ

**Which CRM should I use?**
This is a separate playbook. The short answer: the one you'll actually open every day. LionDesk, Follow Up Boss, kvCORE, and HubSpot are all viable. Your brokerage may provide one. Use that first before paying for a third-party option.

**How many contacts should be in my CRM?**
At minimum: your entire sphere of influence (200+ contacts), every prospect you've spoken with in the last 12 months, and every past client. There's no maximum. The bigger the database, the more valuable it becomes.

**What do I do with contacts who never respond?**
Keep them in the long-term nurture sequence. Real estate decisions are made on the seller's timeline, not yours. Someone who doesn't respond for 18 months may call you when they're ready to sell. Don't prune your database aggressively.
